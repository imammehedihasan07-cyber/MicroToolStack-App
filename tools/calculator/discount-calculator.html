The Mathematical Formulas Behind Discount Calculations

Calculating sales discounts involves straightforward percentage arithmetic:

1. Standard Single Discount Formula

To calculate the monetary savings and reduced price for a single discount percentage:

$$\text{Savings} = \text{Original Price} \times \left(\frac{\text{Discount \%}}{100}\right)$$

$$\text{Final Sale Price} = \text{Original Price} - \text{Savings}$$

Example: An item originally priced at $80 with a 20% discount saves $80 \times 0.20 = $16, resulting in a final sale price of $64.

2. Stacked / Double Discount Calculation Method

Stores often advertise a main discount plus an additional coupon (e.g., 30% Off plus an extra 10% Off). Note that stacked discounts are calculated sequentially rather than added together directly (30% + 10% $\neq$ 40%):

  • Step 1: Apply the first discount to the original price to establish the intermediate price.
  • Step 2: Apply the second discount percentage to that intermediate price to derive the final reduced price.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

How do you calculate a discount percentage?

Multiply the original price by the discount percentage divided by 100 to find your total savings. Subtract savings from the original price to get the final sale price.

Is a 20% discount plus an extra 10% discount equal to 30% off?

No. Stacked discounts apply sequentially. The 10% extra discount is calculated on the already-reduced price after taking 20% off, resulting in a total discount of 28% off the original price.
