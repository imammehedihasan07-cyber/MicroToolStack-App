Understanding Value Added Tax (VAT) and Goods & Services Tax (GST)

Value Added Tax (VAT) and Goods and Services Tax (GST) are consumption taxes levied on goods and services at each stage of the supply chain where value is added. Millions of businesses, accountants, and consumers manage daily sales invoicing involving both tax-exclusive (net) and tax-inclusive (gross) prices.

Our free Online VAT & GST Calculator automates consumption tax mathematics. Whether you are adding 20% UK VAT to a net invoice or extracting 15% GST from a retail receipt, this tool provides instant financial accuracy for business bookkeeping.

Mathematical Formulas for Exclusive and Inclusive Tax Calculations

Calculating consumption taxes depends on whether you are starting with a net or gross price structure:

1. Adding VAT/GST to a Net Price (Exclusive Tax)

When starting with a net price ($N$) and adding tax at rate ($R\%$):

$$\text{Tax Amount} = N \times \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)$$

$$\text{Gross Total} = N + \text{Tax Amount}$$

Example: A net item of $100 with 15% VAT yields $15 in tax, making the total gross price $115.

2. Extracting VAT/GST from a Gross Price (Inclusive Tax)

When starting with a total inclusive gross price ($G$) and finding the embedded tax component:

$$\text{Tax Amount} = G - \left(\frac{G}{1 + \frac{R}{100}}\right)$$

$$\text{Net Price} = G - \text{Tax Amount}$$

Example: A total gross receipt of $115 with 15% inclusive VAT extracts exactly $15 in tax, leaving a net price of $100.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

How do I remove VAT from a total price?

Divide the total gross price by 1 + (Tax Rate / 100) to find the original net price before tax.

What is the difference between VAT and GST?

Both VAT and GST are multi-stage consumption taxes. The primary difference lies in regional naming conventions and administrative frameworks across different countries.
